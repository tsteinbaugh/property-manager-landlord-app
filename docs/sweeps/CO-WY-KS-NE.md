## COLORADO

### edu-confirmed-absences-outside-title-co: **ERROR**
The row says "nothing makes registration a precondition to getting a judgment for possession or to collecting rent." That is false for collecting rent. Two general filings bar a suit to collect a debt: the trade-name statement (C.R.S. § 7-71-102(1)) and the statement of foreign entity authority (§ 7-90-802(1)(a)). Both bars are worded "for the collection of a debt" (or "of its debts"). A possession-only claim may fall outside them, but a claim for rent does not.

- old_str: `Third, there is no statewide landlord registration or rental licence in Colorado, and nothing makes registration a precondition to getting a judgment for possession or to collecting rent.`
- new_str: `Third, there is no statewide landlord registration or rental licence in Colorado, and nothing in the landlord-tenant law makes registration a precondition to getting a judgment for possession. But two general business filings can stop you suing for rent. If you rent under any name other than your own true name, a statement of trade name must be on file with the Secretary of State, and until it is you may not maintain a court case to collect a debt, such as unpaid rent, from anyone you dealt with under that name (C.R.S. §§ 7-71-101, 7-71-102). And an out-of-state company that transacts business here must file a statement of foreign entity authority naming a Colorado registered agent, and until it does it may not sue to collect its debts and the court may stay its case (C.R.S. §§ 7-90-801, 7-90-802, 7-90-803). Owning property "without more" is not transacting business, but whether renting it out is more is a case-law question, so file before you need to sue. Neither failure voids the lease or stops you defending a case.`

- old_str: `No statute requires a landlord to cooperate with, report to, or hand information to law enforcement, no statute requires the tenant to do so, and there is no Colorado crime-free housing addendum law of the kind some states have.`
- new_str: `No statute requires a landlord, as a landlord, to cooperate with or hand information to law enforcement, no statute requires the tenant to do so, and there is no Colorado crime-free housing addendum law of the kind some states have. But two general duties bind you as they bind everyone. Colorado makes it the duty of every person who has reasonable grounds to believe a crime has been committed to report it promptly to law enforcement, and a good-faith report is immune from civil liability; the statute sets no penalty (C.R.S. § 18-8-115). And if you or your staff find a tenant dead and the cause is unknown, or the death may be unnatural, accidental, from drugs or another cause the coroner must look into, you must report it immediately to the police or the coroner, and the body must not be moved before the coroner arrives unless that is needed to identify the person, protect the property, preserve evidence or protect life, health or safety (C.R.S. § 30-10-606(1.2)).`

### Nebraska supporting quotes (nebraskalegislature.gov, read 2026-11-06)
- **§ 21-155(b)** (statutes.php?statute=21-155): "A foreign limited liability company may not transact business in this state until it qualifies with the Secretary of State as provided in sections 21-156 and 21-158. ..."

(7) creating or acquiring indebtedness, mortgages, or security interests in real or personal property; (8) securing or collecting debts or enforcing mortgages ... and holding, protecting, or maintaining property so acquired; ...
