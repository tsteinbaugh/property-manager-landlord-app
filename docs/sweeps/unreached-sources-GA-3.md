### `edu-nonresident-owner-agent-ga`

Verdict: FIX
Reason: The row's statements hold: § 44-7-3(a) and § 44-7-25(a) say what it says, and a search found no withholding on rent a tenant pays. The row leaves out three things a nonresident owner needs: foreign-entity registration and the suit bar, service on an unregistered company, and two Title 48 withholding rules that apply to nonresident owners (not to tenants).

new_str:
```
and a nonresident owner of single-family or duplex rentals must employ a Georgia-licensed broker (see Out-of-State Owners Must Use a Georgia Broker). If you own through an out-of-state LLC or corporation, it must get a certificate of authority from the Secretary of State before transacting business in Georgia, and until it does it cannot bring an eviction or other court case, though its contracts stay valid and it can defend a case (O.C.G.A. §§ 14-11-702, 14-11-711, 14-2-1501, 14-2-1502). Owning property, without more, is not transacting business, so whether renting it out is depends on the facts. An LLC that needs a certificate but has no registered agent here can be served through the Secretary of State, and a foreign corporation with no registered agent can be served by mail to its officers, with a copy to the Secretary of State (O.C.G.A. §§ 14-11-703(h), 14-2-1510(b)). Owning property in Georgia lets Georgia courts hear claims arising from that ownership (O.C.G.A. § 9-10-91). Two tax rules reach nonresident owners. A partnership, S corporation or LLC that owns property in Georgia must withhold Georgia income tax at 4 percent from each nonresident member's share of its Georgia income, unless it files a composite return or another exception applies (O.C.G.A. § 48-7-129). And when a nonresident sells Georgia real property, the buyer generally must withhold 3 percent of the price (O.C.G.A. § 48-7-128).
```
