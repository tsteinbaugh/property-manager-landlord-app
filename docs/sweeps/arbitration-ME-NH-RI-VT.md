## MAINE: HOLD

**Reason.** Maine's Uniform Arbitration Act validates pre-dispute arbitration clauses (14 M.R.S. § 5927) and makes a prior waiver of counsel ineffective (§ 5932), which the clause respects. But Maine attaches penalties to lease terms that waive tenant rights. Under 14 M.R.S. § 6030(1), a landlord who requires a tenant to sign a lease term "that has the effect of waiving a tenant right established in chapter 709, this chapter or chapter 710-A" commits an unfair trade practice under 5 M.R.S. § 207. That carries the tenant's private action with mandatory attorney's fees (5 M.R.S. § 213(1), (2)) and Attorney General civil penalties of up to $10,000 per intentional violation (5 M.R.S. § 209). Chapter 710 gives tenants court-based rights the clause would move to arbitration. The habitability section lets a tenant "file a complaint against the landlord in the District Court or Superior Court" and lists what "the court may" order, including a rent rebate (§ 6021(3), (4)), and it voids any other waiver of "the rights or benefits provided by this section" (§ 6021(5)). The clause keeps every remedy (Mitsubishi), and a ban on giving up court access is the kind of rule Kindred says the FAA displaces. Still, § 6030(1) can be read to reach the clause, and § 6030 puts penalty exposure on including it. There is also a textual signal: the Legislature expressly allowed lease arbitration and jury waivers only for commercial leases (§ 6017(4), (5)). Under the owner's rule, this is a HOLD.

- bodyText:

> Maine's arbitration act makes a written agreement to arbitrate future disputes valid and enforceable, and a party's right to a lawyer cannot be waived in advance (14 M.R.S. §§ 5927, 5932). Maine's rental law points the other way for residential leases, though. Requiring a tenant to sign a lease term that has the effect of waiving any tenant right in Maine's eviction, rental property or security deposit chapters is an unfair trade practice (14 M.R.S. § 6030(1)). Federal arbitration law may override some of these limits, but because Maine attaches penalties to the lease term itself, the library does not offer an arbitration clause for Maine. ## NEW HAMPSHIRE: OFFER

**Reason.** RSA 542:1 makes a written pre-dispute arbitration provision "valid, irrevocable, and enforceable," and its only exclusion is for employer-employee agreements. Chapter 542 has no consumer, residential, real-property or possession exclusion and no notice or format requirement. New Hampshire's lease anti-waiver rules only void the term. RSA 540:28 voids a waiver of rights under the eviction chapter, and the clause's (b)(1) keeps possession, with its defenses, in court. RSA 540-A:8, III voids a waiver of deposit rights, and the clause's (b)(3) keeps deposit claims in court. RSA 358-A:10, I voids a waiver of Consumer Protection Act damages, and the clause's (c) keeps every remedy. No statute attaches a penalty to including a void term (edu-no-prohibited-term-penalty-nh). The only such rule, RSA 354-A:13, III, concerns discriminatory conveyance and occupancy restrictions. No statute bans lease jury waivers (edu-no-jury-waiver-rule-nh). One rule could be read against the clause: RSA 540-A:4, II gives a tenant "the right to seek relief ... by filing a petition in the district" court for lockouts, utility shutoffs and similar violations. Applied to require a courtroom, that is a court-access rule of the kind Kindred says the FAA displaces, and it carries no penalty for the lease term. The clause also leaves a 540-A petition's emergency and temporary orders in court under (b)(6). Two limits apply. First, RSA 542:1 reaches a controversy "arising out of such contract," so occupancy claims outside the lease rest on the FAA ("arising out of such contract or transaction," 9 U.S.C. § 2). Second, the general Consumer Protection Act (RSA 358-A:2, :10) could in theory be argued against a landlord who uses an unenforceable term, but no statute says so, and case law was not read.

## RHODE ISLAND: HOLD

**Reason.** Rhode Island's Arbitration Act validates a "clearly written and expressed" pre-dispute provision (R.I. Gen. Laws § 10-3-2(a)). Since 2024 the Act also expressly regulates consumer arbitration: a non-waivable right to counsel (§ 10-3-22), and fee-payment default rules with mandatory sanctions on the drafting party (§§ 10-3-23 to 10-3-25). So state arbitration law accepts consumer arbitration, provided the landlord pays the fees on time. The Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, though, bars a rental agreement term by which the tenant "Agrees to waive or forego rights or remedies under this chapter" (§ 34-18-17(a)(1)). If a landlord deliberately uses an agreement containing provisions known to be prohibited, the tenant may recover actual damages plus up to three months' periodic rent and attorney's fees (§ 34-18-17(b)). The Act also gives the district or housing court jurisdiction "with respect to any claim arising from a transaction subject to this chapter" (§ 34-18-9), and § 34-18-17(a)(1) can be read to reach a clause that sends those claims out of that court. A reading that requires a courtroom is the kind of rule Kindred says the FAA displaces, but the penalty attaches to the lease term itself. Under the owner's rule, this is a HOLD. Note: § 34-18-16, named in the brief, concerns unsigned rental agreements and is not relevant. § 34-18-15(a) permits terms "not prohibited by this chapter or other rule of law," which leads back to § 34-18-17.

## VERMONT: HOLD

**Reason.** Vermont's arbitration act validates pre-dispute agreements (12 V.S.A. § 5652(a)) but makes them unenforceable without a signed "ACKNOWLEDGMENT OF ARBITRATION" in the statutory form (§ 5652(b)), which the shared clause does not contain. A prior waiver of counsel is ineffective (§ 5655), and no arbitration agreement may stop a person from going to court to enforce constitutional or civil rights (§ 5653(b)). The acknowledgment rule singles out arbitration on its face (compare Casarotto), so the FAA would likely displace it where the FAA applies. The clause's fallback to state law would still fail without it. The deciding problem is Vermont's standard-form contract statute. In a standard-form contract between a drafting party and an individual who could not negotiate, which describes a business landlord's form lease, "A waiver of the individual's right to a jury trial or to bring a class action" is presumed substantively unconscionable (9 V.S.A. § 6055(a)(2)). If the drafting party "seeks to enforce" such a term and it is found unconscionable, the court may find an unfair and deceptive practice under § 2453 and award up to $1,000 in statutory damages per term plus costs and attorney's fees (§ 6055(d)). The clause expressly gives up "THE RIGHT TO HAVE A JUDGE OR JURY DECIDE." Under Kindred, a jury-protecting rule applied to arbitration's defining feature is displaced by the FAA, but § 6055 attaches money exposure to a jury-waiver-type term. Separately, 9 V.S.A. § 4454 voids any lease provision that "attempts to circumvent ... obligations and remedies" of the rental chapter, though it carries no penalty of its own. Under the owner's rule, this is a HOLD. Note: the brief's "9 V.S.A. § 4446" does not exist; chapter 137 runs §§ 4451 to 4475, and the anti-circumvention section is § 4454.

- title: Arbitration Clauses in Vermont Leases: Why the Library Does Not Offer One
- bodyText:

> Vermont's arbitration act makes a written agreement to arbitrate future disputes valid and enforceable, but only if the parties also sign a prominently displayed "Acknowledgment of Arbitration" in the form the statute sets out (12 V.S.A. § 5652). A right to a lawyer cannot be waived in advance, and no arbitration agreement can stop anyone from going to court over constitutional or civil rights (§§ 5653(b), 5655). Two other laws matter for leases. Your lease may not contain any provision that attempts to circumvent the rental chapter's obligations and remedies (9 V.S.A. § 4454). In a standard-form lease that an individual tenant couldn't negotiate, a waiver of the tenant's right to a jury trial or to bring a class action is presumed unconscionable. If you try to enforce one and a court finds it unconscionable, it may treat that as an unfair and deceptive practice and award up to $1,000 per term plus attorney's fees (§ 6055). An arbitration clause gives up a jury. Federal arbitration law may override some of these rules, but because Vermont attaches money damages to enforcing such a term, the library does not offer an arbitration clause for Vermont. Vermont case law was not reviewed.
