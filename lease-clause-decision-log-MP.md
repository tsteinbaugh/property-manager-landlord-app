### §2.2 Class decisions, and clauses read in the main pass and not tagged
**Class decisions** (cited in delivered notes as "log §2.2 class decision N"):
1. **No general residential landlord-tenant act.** The Holdover Tenancy Act (2 CMC §§ 40201-40210) is the only landlord-tenant chapter, and § 40201 keeps the parties free to 'create additional rights, duties, and obligations in and by virtue of a rental agreement'. A shared clause that only sets contract terms is lawful unless some other written law (or the cautious Restatement course in 2) cuts against it. A topic is "Present" only where a Commonwealth statute, regulation, court rule, constitutional provision or federal law that reaches the CNMI says something on it.
2. **Rules of decision (7 CMC § 3401).** Where no written law speaks, the Restatements are the rules of decision, but only 'in the absence of written law or local customary law to the contrary' (7 CMC § 3401); local custom (for example the customary land rules in 8 CMC §§ 2902-2905) prevails over them. The Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant was not read (not in the corpus) and case law was not searched. Cautious course in rows: assume a residential landlord owes a duty to keep the dwelling fit to live in, may not retaliate against a tenant for lawful complaints, and can't rely on a lease clause that waives either or that exempts the landlord from liability for its own negligence. Rows say "Restatement not read; case law not searched". Never describe Restatement content as read or quote it.
3. **Deposits (4 CMC § 5171).** Treat every landlord as a "business" covered by § 5171 (cautious; "business" undefined in § 5171; the tax title's definition, 'any regular and continuous activity carried on by a person for the purpose of earning a profit' (4 CMC § 1103(b)), points the same way for a landlord who rents for profit; NMIAC § 155-10.1-335 treats residential rentals as commercial occupancies). Refund 'the balance of the security deposit within ten days after the property is returned' — ten calendar days (operative text; the Commission comment's findings say business days; NMI R. Civ. P. 6(a)(1)(D)'s weekend exclusion for periods of 10 days or less may apply in court, but the cheaper error is calendar days). Fine up to $1,000 per violation (§ 5173). No other deposit rule (cap, interest, account, itemization, deduction list, walk-through) — each needs its own battery before a confirmed-absent row. A shared clause whose only figure is "the time required by applicable law" gets an MP override stating ten days (AS 5).
4. **Termination and default notices.** Periodic tenancies end on written notice before the end of a period: year 60 days, quarter 30, month 15, week 7 (§ 40203) 'Unless otherwise agreed' — a lease may set a different period. Nonpayment: three days' written notice containing a statement 'in substantially the following form' (the statutory text), served by hand delivery or by leaving a copy at the premises if the tenant is absent (§ 40204(b)); the lease is not invited to change the form, period or service. Other material breaches: the lease may set its own cure period and notice method; if it is silent, 15 days' written notice to cure or quit, hand-delivered or left at the premises (§ 40204(c)). No rule makes any ground "no-cure" except the end of the term (§ 40204(a)). Holdover after termination without permission: double the rent for the period of refusal (§ 40205). The prevailing party 'may recover reasonable court costs, including attorney's fees, from the nonprevailing party' in any civil action under the chapter (§ 40209). The statutory route to possession is the Superior Court summary procedure: the landlord 'may recover possession of the premises in the manner provided in 2 CMC § 40206' (§ 40205), and § 40201 keeps other rights 'in addition'. No statute authorizes self-help; whether the common law allows it is a Restatement and case-law question not read. Cautious course in rows: recover possession only through the court and never lock out, remove belongings or cut utilities.
5. **Discrimination.** CFHA classes (2 CMC § 40103): race, color, religion, sex, familial status, marital status, disability, national origin; exemptions § 40102(b), § 40106. Plus, cautiously, 4 CMC § 5120 for every landlord (treated as a "merchant"): also creed, age, ethnic background, citizenship, place of birth or residence. Federal FHA (42 U.S.C. § 3604; "State" includes territories, § 3602(g)). Shared clauses that screen or set terms by any of these are untagged. Senior housing: the CFHA (2 CMC § 40106(b)) and the federal FHA (42 U.S.C. § 3607(b)) exempt qualifying 55+/62+ housing from the familial-status rules, but § 5120 bars a merchant's age discrimination with no such exception; cautious course: don't restrict by age without advice (case law not searched). No NMHC regulation implementing § 40106(b)(2)(iii) was found.
6. **Consumer Protection Act (4 CMC §§ 5101-5123) treated as reaching residential leasing** (cautious; "commerce" includes property 'real, personal'; case law not searched). A clause must not 'Represent[] that a transaction confers or involves rights, remedies, or obligations which it does not confer or involve, or which are prohibited by law' (§ 5105(bb)) and must not be 'an unconscionable provision' (§ 5105(gg)). So a shared clause that misstates a tenant's rights under CNMI or federal law, or a one-sided penalty a court could call unconscionable, is untagged or overridden. Private action: actual damages, equal liquidated damages for wilful violations, fees (§ 5112).
7. **Land alienation (N.M.I. Const. art. XII).** Any person may take a residential lease of 55 years or less including renewal rights. A lease clause giving a tenant renewal or extension rights that could carry the total over 55 years, or a purchase option or right of first refusal on a freehold, works for a non-NMD tenant only as far as Art. XII allows; such a transaction 'shall be void'. Shared option/renewal clauses are tagged only if their total term can't exceed 55 years and they grant no freehold; any purchase-option clause carries an NMD condition. A landlord corporation or an LLC is irrelevant to an ordinary lease (the restriction is on acquisition, i.e. the tenant's side).
8. **Writing and recording (2 CMC §§ 4912-4915).** A lease for more than one year must be a written instrument 'subscribed by the party creating, granting' it (§ 4912), and an agreement not to be performed within a year must be in writing (§ 4914(a)). Whether § 4913(a) requires a residential lease for more than one year to be recorded by 'the party receiving the interest' is unsettled (is a lease a "conveyance or other transfer"? case law not searched); cautious course: record it; § 4915(b) fine up to $10,000 for a knowing and wilful violation of § 4913. Recording act (1 CMC §§ 3701-3706, re-enacted by PL 21-39): 'All documents that must be recorded, including transfers, leases exceeding one year, and encumbrances affecting real property, must be acknowledged before a notary public' (1 CMC § 3704), though lack of acknowledgment doesn't affect validity between the parties; and an unrecorded interest 'other than a lease for a term not exceeding one year' is not valid against a later good-faith purchaser or mortgagee who records first (1 CMC § 3705(a)). Cautious course for a lease over one year: sign it before a notary and record it. Spousal consent: 'The spouse with the right to management and control of real property must obtain the written consent of the other spouse in order to sell, convey or lease for more than one year any real property in which the other spouse has any legal or equitable interest.' (8 CMC § 1821(d)); without it the lease is voidable by the nonconsenting spouse, who may sue the grantor spouse or the tenant (§ 1821(e)). Cautious course: a married landlord gets the spouse's written consent (or signature) on a lease over one year. Customary land: 'Unless the family consents or agrees otherwise, family land shall not be passed on by will, devised, sold, leased, exchanged, mortgaged, partitioned, or otherwise disposed of by the customary trustee.' (8 CMC § 2904(c), Carolinian custom; see also § 2905(d)); cautious course: when the landlord holds land as a customary trustee, get the family's written consent to the lease.
9. **Exculpation.** No CNMI statute voids a lease clause limiting the landlord's liability; under class decision 2 the cautious course is not to rely on one for the landlord's own negligence. Library choice: tag the no-disclaimer variants (`tenants-property-insurance-ks-oh-ca`, `parking-ks-oh-ca`, `storage-space-ks-oh-ca`) and leave the base disclaimers untagged.
10. **Fees and interest.** No statute lists or caps rental fees (application, late, pet, move-in, key, cleaning) — each topic needs its own battery. Interest: no action lies for interest above 1% a month where the contract involves a principal sum over $300 (2% a month where the principal sum is $300 or less) (4 CMC § 5301); receiving more than 2% a month 'for or upon the loan or forbearance to enforce the payment of money' is a crime (§ 5303). Whether a late fee is "interest" or forbearance consideration under §§ 5301-5303 is unsettled (case law not searched); cautious course: keep any interest on unpaid amounts at or below 1% a month and keep a late fee a one-time, reasonable charge. Third-party collectors may not collect an incidental charge, fee or expense 'unless the interest or incidental charge, fee, or expense is expressly authorized by the agreement creating the obligation and is legally chargeable to the debtor, or is otherwise legally chargeable' (4 CMC § 51302(e)(4)).
11. **Price freeze (4 CMC § 5142).** During a Governor's price freeze it is a felony to increase 'housing rentals including apartments and condos'. Any clause that raises rent automatically or lets the landlord raise it (renewal increase, escalation, mid-term increase, holdover rate stated as a rent increase) must yield to a freeze: shared rent-increase clauses are tagged only if they can't operate during a freeze, or get an MP override adding "except while a Governor's price freeze under 4 CMC § 5142 is in effect". EO 2026-020 (Sept. 23, 2026) continued the EO 2026-005 freeze for Rota 'consistent with the declaration of a state of significant emergency for Typhoon Bavi by Executive Order 2026-012'. A freeze lasts until rescinded or until the declaration ends (4 CMC § 5142(a)), and a significant-emergency declaration ends 'automatically after thirty days unless renewed by the Governor' (1 CMC § 20144(g)(2)); EO 2026-012 and any renewal were not reached, so rows say the Rota freeze was continued on Sept. 23, 2026 and tell landlords to check the Governor's current orders.
12. **Electronic notices.** No CNMI electronic-transactions act (MP-M-ET02). Federal E-SIGN applies to the CNMI (15 U.S.C. § 7006(12)), but its validation does not reach notices of 'default, acceleration, repossession, foreclosure, or eviction, or the right to cure, under a credit agreement secured by, or a rental agreement for, a primary residence of an individual' (15 U.S.C. § 7003(b)(2)(B)); the 3-day nonpayment notice must be hand-delivered or left at the premises (§ 40204(b)). Shared e-notice clauses are tagged only if they exclude those notices.
13. **Government landlords.** The Holdover Tenancy Act doesn't apply to a lease with the CNMI government, its agencies or instrumentalities as a party (§ 40201), and the CPA exempts government transactions (§ 5106(a)). Rows are written for private landlords; NMHC public-housing and Section 8 rules (NMIAC Title 100) are flagged, not resolved.
14. **Building Safety annual certificate (NMIAC § 155-10.1-335).** Apartments and residential rentals (including single-family houses) need an annual Certificate of Compliance after a Building Safety inspection, posted conspicuously; failure 'raised issues of non-habitability'. This is a landlord duty (education), not lease text.
15. **Municipal and senatorial-district law** (Saipan, Tinian and Aguiguan, Rota local laws; zoning, e.g. NMIAC ch. 165-30.1 Saipan zoning): flag, don't resolve (SOP rule 3).

### §6.3 Legal and drafting judgments (rule 76; made, not asked)
- **Deposit refund period:** ten calendar days, not business days (4 CMC § 5171's operative 'within ten days'; the Commission comment's findings say business days; NMI R. Civ. P. 6(a)(1)(D) may exclude weekends in court). The cheaper error for a landlord is calendar days (class decision 3).
- **Every landlord is a "business" under § 5171 and a "merchant" under the Consumer Protection Act** (cautious; class decisions 3, 5, 6; 4 CMC § 1103(b)'s tax definition points the same way for a landlord who rents for profit).
- **Restatement as rules of decision (7 CMC § 3401):** the Restatement (Second) of Property: Landlord and Tenant was not read; rows assume the habitability duty and anti-retaliation rule apply and don't rely on waivers or exculpation for the landlord's own negligence (class decision 2).
- **Holdover rate:** `holdover-mp` reads 'double the amount of rent due' as a total of twice the rent for the holdover days ("in place of, not in addition to"), the cheaper reading for the landlord; case law not searched.
- **Price freeze:** rent-increase clauses carry an exception "while a Governor's price freeze under 4 CMC § 5142 is in effect"; a fee or reimbursement fixed at signing is not a rent increase during a later freeze (`government-fee-reimbursement-mp`).
- **Self-help:** no statute authorizes a lockout, removal of belongings or utility cut-off, and § 40205 says the landlord 'may recover' possession through § 40206; whether the common law allows self-help was not read, so rows give the cautious course (never use it) and say so (class decision 4, corrected after check round 1).
- **Foreclosure:** a mortgage is foreclosed only through the court (2 CMC § 4531) with a 12-month redemption and the buyer collecting rents; a deed of trust under the Deed of Trust Act (2 CMC §§ 4611-4637, for federal-agency financing) may allow a trustee's sale without a court case and with no redemption (§§ 4621, 4622, 4630) — found by check round 1 and corrected in `edu-foreclosure-mp` and `edu-foreclosure-disclosure-mp`.
